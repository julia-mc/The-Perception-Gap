# Statistical Methods

## ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)
Used to determine whether average perception gap rates differ across police districts.

**Key finding:**
- Perception gap rates differ significantly across districts.

## Tukey HSD Post-Hoc Analysis
Used after ANOVA to identify which specific districts differ from each other.

**Key findings:**
- Districts 1 and 2 generally had higher perception gap rates.
- Districts 5 and 6 generally had lower perception gap rates.
- Several district comparisons were statistically significant.


## Linear Regression
Used to evaluate the relationship between police district, year, and total perception gap rates.

**Key findings:**
- Approximately 24% of the variation in total perception gap rate was explained by district and year.
- Gap rates generally trended downward over time.
- District-level differences remained consistent with ANOVA findings.


## Logistic Regression — No Report Outcome
Used to predict whether a dispatch call ended without a matching crime report.

**Key findings:**
- Service / non-criminal calls were much more likely to result in no-report outcomes.
- Higher severity calls were less likely to end without a report.
- Nighttime calls were more likely to produce no-report outcomes.

## Logistic Regression — Classification Mismatch
Used to predict whether the dispatch classification differed from the final crime classification.

**Key findings:**
- Service calls were much less likely to produce classification mismatches.
- The perception gap appears to be driven more by calls that never become formal crime reports than by classification mismatches.


# Major Findings
- Perception gap rates differ significantly across police districts.
- Districts 1 and 2 generally showed higher perception gap rates than districts 5 and 6.
- Gap rates decreased over several years compared to 2017.
- Service / non-criminal calls are a major contributor to perception gap outcomes.
- The perception gap is driven primarily by no report outcomes rather than by differences in severity classification.


# Final Conclusion
- The analysis suggests that the perception gap is not primarily caused by incorrect crime classification. Instead, the gap is largely driven by calls that never become formal crime reports, particularly service and non criminal incidents.
- The findings indicate that district-level and situational factors influence perception gap patterns and may help guide future public safety and dispatch policy analysis.
